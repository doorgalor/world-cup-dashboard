What the data says

The map: a tournament bigger than its 48 teams

32 of the 48 teams reached the knockouts. Turn on player concentration and you'll see the real map of the World Cup: 27 countries that didn't qualify still had players there through their clubs. Clubs in England employed 204 of the 1,248 squad players, more than Germany and Spain combined.

Player profiles: Mbappé led, Messi defied age

Mbappé finished top scorer with 10 goals and 3 assists. At 38, Messi scored 8 and set up 4 on the way to the final, ranking in the top 6% of forwards for non-penalty goals per 90. Bellingham's 7 goals came at 22.

Where players play beats where they're from

A strong home league barely predicted a deep run. The share of a squad at top-five-league clubs did, almost as well as FIFA ranking. Spain had all 26 and won. The exceptions are the stories: Canada, Egypt, Mexico and Paraguay reached the round of 16 with six or fewer such players each. Germany had 25 of 26 and went out in the round of 32.

Europe's big five still supply most of the World Cup

The top five leagues supplied 538 of 1,248 players (43%), led by the Premier League with 196. Further down, the Saudi Pro League (47) and MLS (45) each supplied more players than the Championship (36).

Most attackers went quieter for their country

118 of 183 midfielders and forwards (64%) produced less xG + xA per 90 at the World Cup than for their clubs, and the typical player fell from 0.36 to 0.25. So a drop alone isn't a bad tournament, but the size of it can be. Mikel Merino bucked it most (+0.68), while Patrik Schick dropped from 0.90 to 0.11.

Lamine Yamal: busy, but not dangerous

12 of 14 stats fell below his Barcelona level. He took the 7th most shots of 57 forwards, but only 13 had worse chances (0.06 xG per shot, against 0.11 for Barcelona). He then scored 1 from 1.7 xG, after beating his xG at club level (16 from 12.8). Spain won anyway, and not through finishing either: its players scored 13 goals from 15.8 xG.
